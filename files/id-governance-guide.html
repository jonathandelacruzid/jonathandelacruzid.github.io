Start Here: The 10 Rules

If you read nothing else, follow these. Everything after this page explains how.

1. No ClickUp task, generate ticket. Every request starts with the ClickUp intake form.

2. Size it first. Every project gets a Tier (1, 2, or 3), and the tier sets the timeline and how much process applies.

3. Objectives before content. Measurable objectives come first; the storyboard is approved before anything is

built.

4. Build from templates. Start every deck, module, and video from the approved templates.

5. Nothing launches without a Go. Content passes the Go/No-Go check before learners see it.

6. Launched is not done. Projects close only when the Definition of Done is met.

7. Name and file it right. Especially video: short names, the right folders, finals only in the Published Library.

8. AI drafts, you decide. Use approved accounts, follow the data rules, and verify everything.

9. Measure past the smile sheet. Plan how you will show behavior change or business impact.

10. Every live course has an owner and a review date.

2. Intake and Prioritization

2.1 Intake (ClickUp)

All requests come through the ClickUp intake form, which creates a task in the Intake list. That task is the single

source of truth until closure. Required fields: requester and sponsor, business problem (what people do now vs.

what they need to do), audience and headcount, the metric it should move, deadline and what drives it, SMEs and

sources, and compliance flag. The ID Lead acknowledges within 2 business days.

2.2 Priority (CPTM Quadrant Module)

Score approved intakes on impact, urgency, reach, effort, and training fit (is this a knowledge/skill gap, or a

process or tool problem training won’t fix?). Align to your CPTM module’s criteria. Decisions go back to the

requester within 5 business days.

Quadrant Profile Decision

Quick Wins High impact, low effort Approve; schedule next.

Strategic High impact, high effort Approve and plan as Tier 3.

Fill-ins Low impact, low effort Do when capacity allows, or offer a self-serve template.

Reconsider Low impact, high effort Decline or redirect to a non-training fix; note why in ClickUp.

5. Ready to Release and Done

5.1 Go/No-Go: Is It Ready?

Held at Gate 5 as a 15-minute check (or an approval comment in ClickUp for Tier 1). The ID shows the evidence,

the ID Lead recommends, the sponsor decides. It is ready only when every line is true:

☐ Accurate: final SME sign-off recorded. (SME)

☐ Sound: every objective has practice and an assessment; peer review done. (Peer)

☐ Accessible: accessibility checklist passed; captions verified. (ID)

☐ Works: launches, tracks, and scores correctly in staging on required devices. (Learning Tech)

☐ Clean: no open Critical or High issues. (ID Lead)

☐ Launch-ready: enrollment set, comms approved, facilitators prepped. (ID)

☐ Measurable: evaluation survey live and follow-up checkpoints scheduled in ClickUp. (ID)

Go = all pass. Go with conditions = only Medium/Low issues left, each with an owner and a fix date within 2

weeks. No-Go = anything else; set a new review date. For Tier 3, move from pilot to full release when the pilot

averages 4.0/5 or higher on reaction, 80%+ pass the assessment first try, and no Critical issues surface (adjust to

your context).

Deadline vs. readiness

A deadline never justifies launching with Critical or High issues. Give the sponsor three options: launch a

smaller scope that is ready, launch in phases, or move the date. Record the choice in ClickUp.

5.2 Definition of Done: When Is the Project Closed?

Launched is not done. Move the ClickUp task to Closed only when:

☐ Content is live and tracking is verified with real learner data.

☐ Deliverables and source files are saved in the right folders (videos per Section 9) and linked on the task.

6. Instructional Design Standards

6.1 Design Principles

•

Backward design: outcome first, then evidence of learning, then activities.

•

Manage cognitive load: explicit, guided instruction and worked examples for novices; chunk content and

cut anything that doesn’t serve an objective (Kirschner, Sweller & Clark).

•

Retrieval and spacing: make learners recall, and spread practice over time with knowledge checks and

follow-ups (Kang).

•

Realistic practice with feedback: use scenarios from the learner’s actual job.

6.2 Needs Assessment (Analysis)

A one-page brief that answers: What are people doing now vs. what they need to do? Why (knowledge/skill, or

process, tools, motivation)? Who exactly is the audience? What metric shows it worked, and what is the baseline?

If the cause isn’t a skill gap, recommend the non-training fix.

6.3 Learning Objectives

Use ABCD: Audience, Behavior (observable verb; never “understand” or “know”), Condition, Degree. Every

objective maps to one practice activity and one assessment item; content that maps to no objective gets cut or

moved to a job aid.

Example: Given an order discrepancy report, new warehouse leads will identify the root cause using the 5

Whys, with no more than one incorrect cause across three practice cases.

6.4 Storyboards

Approved at Gate 3 before building. Columns: Screen ID, Objective, On-screen text, Narration, Visuals,

Interaction/Check, Accessibility notes, SME comments.

6.5 Video Production Standards

•

2–6 minutes per video; split longer topics into a series. SME-approved script before recording.

•

1080p minimum, 16:9, clean audio; edited in Adobe Premiere Pro (Section 10).

•

Human-verified captions and a transcript on every video; approved intro, outro, and lower thirds.

•

AI voices, avatars, or generated footage need ID Lead approval and disclosure (Section 11).

12. Measurement and Evaluation

Set the success metric in Analysis and the evaluation plan in Design, so measurement is built in, not bolted on.

Kirkpatrick level Measures How When

1. Reaction Satisfaction, relevance Post-session survey Immediately

2. Learning Knowledge and skill Assessments, pre/post End of course

3. Behavior On-the-job use Manager check, follow-up survey 30–90 days

4. Results Business impact KPIs from the needs assessment 90–180 days

LTEM tiers: 1 Attendance, 2 Activity, 3 Learner Perceptions, 4 Knowledge, 5 Decision Competence, 6 Task

Competence, 7 Transfer, 8 Effects of Transfer. Minimum target: Tier 1 projects reach LTEM 3+; Tier 2 skill-based

projects reach 5+; Tier 3 programs measure 7–8. Tiers 1–3 alone don’t prove learning worked.

ROE (did we meet the sponsor’s expectations from intake?) for Tier 2 and 3. ROI = (net benefits ÷ costs) × 100,

for Tier 3 when benefits can be put in dollars.

VILT data to capture after each live session: attendance, chat comments, breakout participation, poll responses,

hand raises, and the session evaluation survey.

Dashboard (monthly): intake volume and on-time delivery, completions and scores, Level 3–4 results, and

courses past their review date. Before reporting, check the data is accurate, relevant to the metric, current, and

anonymized.
