One AI and instructional design standard for a supply chain company and an EdTech startup, curated over a year and a half to keep what matters and drop what doesn’t.

The context

Two companies, one standard.

I design learning for a supply chain company and an EdTech startup, and both needed AI governance in place: which tools to use for what, what data can go in, and how a request becomes a finished, measurable course. Each company works from its own tailored guide; alongside them, I built one master standard and refined it over a year and a half of real projects, keeping what protected quality and cutting what only added work. With many projects running at once, it had to stay light enough to actually follow.

The 10 Rules

If you read nothing else, follow these. The guide is written as the ideal, a perfect-world version, but it’s the standard every project is held to. It leans toward the startup, where I have more room to make changes.

  1. No ClickUp task, generate ticket.Every request starts with the ClickUp intake form.
  2. Size it first.Every project gets a Tier (1, 2 or 3), and the tier sets the timeline and how much process applies.
  3. Objectives before content.Measurable objectives come first; the storyboard is approved before anything is built.
  4. Build from templates.Every deck, module and video starts from an approved template.
  5. Nothing launches without a Go.Content passes the Go/No-Go check before learners see it.
  6. Launched is not done.Projects close only when the Definition of Done is met.
  7. Name and file it right.Especially video: short names, the right folders, finals only in the Published Library.
  8. AI drafts, you decide.Use approved accounts, follow the data rules and verify everything.
  9. Measure past the smile sheet.Plan how you’ll show behavior change or business impact.
  10. Every live course has an owner and a review date.

Right-sized process

Three tiers

Quick turn

Job aids, updates, one-pagers, short videos. 1–2 weeks on a light path: notes in the task, a peer and SME check, an accessibility check.

Standard

eLearning modules, 1–2 hour ILT or VILT, video series. 4–8 weeks through the full lifecycle and every gate.

Strategic

Curricula, onboarding, certification, blended programs. 8–16+ weeks, with biweekly sponsor check-ins and a pilot.

AI governance · AI drafts, you decide

The right tool for the job

Copilot for work inside Microsoft 365. Claude for long sources, objectives, storyboards and QA second reads. ChatGPT for brainstorming and concept images.

Clear data rules

Four classes (public, internal, confidential and restricted) decide what can go into which account. Restricted data never goes into any AI tool.

Verify everything

Every output is checked for accuracy, real citations, alignment to objectives, bias, accessibility and brand voice before it’s used.

Log every use

Each AI use goes in the project’s AI usage log. If data ends up somewhere it shouldn’t, the ID Lead and IT hear about it the same day.
